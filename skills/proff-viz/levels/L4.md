## Show it, and build it from the ground (every L4 output)
- **Never ask the reader to picture it or to already know it.** If an idea has a shape, a count, or a step, draw it or compute it. Do not write "imagine", "picture this", "clearly", "obviously", "intuitively", or "as you know"; put the drawing or the numbers there instead. The page audit flags these words.
- **Build the ladder before the target.** The target idea is made of smaller ideas. List them down to one the reader can check by counting. The first scenes climb it one rung at a time; the target appears only after.
- Each rung: a tiny example with real numbers first, then the general form. A symbol appears only after the example it names.
- Example, maximum likelihood (θ = chance of heads):
  1. probability of one outcome: one toss, P(H) = 0.7
  2. joint probability: independent tosses multiply, so P(H, H, T) = 0.7 × 0.7 × 0.3 = 0.147
  3. likelihood: keep the data (H, H, T) fixed and slide θ: L(θ) = θ²(1 − θ); L(0.5) = 0.125, L(0.9) = 0.081
  4. the peak: L is largest at θ = 2/3 (L = 0.148), the maximum likelihood estimate
  5. logs: many small products underflow; log L turns the product into a sum with the same peak
  6. calculus: the slope of log L is 0 at the peak, which gives θ̂ = heads / tosses; the same recipe gives the normal mean
- Pages: the first scenes are the rungs. Calc: the first cells are the simplest rung. Deck: facts cards climb the ladder.
- Skip a rung only when the user shows they have it (they say so, or answer a check). Say which rungs you skipped.
